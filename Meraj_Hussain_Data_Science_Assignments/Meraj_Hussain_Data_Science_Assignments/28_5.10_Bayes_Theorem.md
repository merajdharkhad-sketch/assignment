## Task 1: Formula and terms
Bayes' theorem is:
P(A | B) = [P(B | A) × P(A)] / P(B)

- **Prior P(A):** chance of A before seeing the new evidence.
- **Likelihood P(B | A):** chance of seeing evidence B if A is true.
- **Evidence P(B):** overall chance of seeing B.
- **Posterior P(A | B):** updated chance of A after evidence B.

For spam detection, A can mean “email is spam” and B can mean “email contains the word offer.”

## Task 2: Spam email example
Out of 1,000 emails, 200 are spam, so P(spam) = 0.20. Suppose the word “offer” appears in 70% of spam emails and 10% of non-spam emails.
P(offer) = (0.70 × 0.20) + (0.10 × 0.80) = 0.22.
P(spam | offer) = (0.70 × 0.20) / 0.22 = 0.6364.
So an email containing “offer” has about a 63.64% chance of being spam under these assumed rates.

## Task 3: Rapid test example
Suppose prevalence is 5%, sensitivity is 98%, and specificity is 95%.
P(COVID | positive) = (0.98 × 0.05) / [(0.98 × 0.05) + (0.05 × 0.95)]
= 0.049 / 0.0965 ≈ 50.78%.
Even with a sensitive test, a positive result does not automatically mean the person has the disease because prevalence and false positives matter. This is a mathematical example, not medical advice.

## Task 4: Bayes theorem in an app
Gmail can update its estimate that a message is spam after checking features such as suspicious links and known spam phrases. The prior is the starting spam rate; the likelihood describes how often those features occur in spam; the posterior is the updated spam estimate after seeing the message.
